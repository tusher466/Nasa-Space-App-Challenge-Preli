Model coordinates do not represent whole-region averages. NASA affiliation or endorsement is not implied. Coastlines: Natural Earth, public domain.

01

Measure the direction and size

A linear fit estimates the annual rate of change. The dashboard expresses the slope per decade. Change over period is slope multiplied by the interval, not a raw difference between two noisy endpoints.

y = intercept + slope × year + residual

Mann–Kendall instead tests monotonic change; its magnitude is estimated using the median of all pairwise slopes (Sen's slope).

02

Account for uncertainty

The default linear test uses Newey–West HAC covariance with Bartlett weights, a data-length-based lag, and a finite-sample scaling factor. It allows short-range residual dependence; p-values and confidence bands use an asymptotic normal approximation.

Ordinary OLS uses Student's t with n − 2 degrees of freedom and assumes independent, constant-variance residuals. Mann–Kendall uses a tie-corrected, continuity-corrected normal approximation and assumes independent annual observations.

03

Control multiple comparisons

Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted q-values are computed across all eligible locations in the selected variable and time interval. “Significant” means q ≤ α, not just p ≤ α.

The procedure controls the false discovery rate under independence or appropriate positive dependence. Spatial correlation can affect its guarantee. Changing the period or method changes the testing family.

04

Interpret with care

Statistical significance is evidence against a zero or absent trend under the selected assumptions. It does not measure practical importance, identify a cause, or prove that change will continue.

At least eight annual observations are required. Gaps are retained, not interpolated. Lag-based HAC treats the remaining observations as successive, so missing years trigger a caution. Residual lag-1 correlation is shown as a diagnostic, not a guarantee of valid inference.
